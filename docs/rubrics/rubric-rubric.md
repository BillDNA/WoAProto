# Rubric rubric

## Purpose

If the graders are wrong, everything is wrong. This rubric keeps the other
rubrics compact, on-topic, and speaking the same paradigm — so one rubric never
grows into an orientation doc and no two rubrics measure the same thing two
different ways.

**Measure with the axes; aim for the goals.**

## Goals

* ==**don't do someone else's job**== — know what the other artifacts are, and make sure the rubric under review isn't grading work another rubric owns (or work the runnable balance checks own).
* ==**ask the right questions**== — axes are not goals; they are a frame for asking *are we approaching the goals*, not a checklist of the goals themselves.
* ==**the cog must work in the machine**== — a rubric milled to a different standard than the rest grinds the machine: it must use this game's vocabulary (`CONTEXT.md`) and this set's findings paradigm.

## Axes of evaluation

### Per rubric

1. ==**Brevity is the soul of wit.**== Does every sentence change how a grader would grade? A rubric earns its length — guidance a grader can hold in their head in one read.
2. ==**Guidance is generic, not specific.**== Would this axis fire on an artifact that didn't exist when it was written? A rubric names the quality it wants, not the instances it has seen — no measured figures, no file paths, no specific card/map names baked into the axis.
3. ==**Axes are orthogonal.**== Do the axes probe genuinely different ways the artifact can fail? There's no point measuring once in feet and again in metres.
4. ==**Goals are not axes.**== Did an axis just restate a goal? A clean 1:1 between a goal and an axis is the thing to avoid.
5. ==**Judgment, not tally.**== Do the axes ask the grader to observe and describe — to produce **findings** tied to the goal each threatens — rather than to score? A number can be optimised for and still be bad; taste can't be tallied. Anything that reduces to a numeric band or a boolean pass/fail belongs in `docs/balance/`, not here.

### Across the set

6. ==**A rubric for every matter of taste.**== Is there something that needs judging but can't be measured directly — some question of *what is good* the runnable checks can't answer? Then the set needs a rubric for it, and only for it.
