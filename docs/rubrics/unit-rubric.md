# Unit rubric

A Unit is a mobile combat piece in one of three roles — Infantry (the line),
Cavalry (the fragile striker), Artillery (the support piece). This rubric grades
the one matter of taste the stats table can't settle: is each Unit a piece a
player *wants* — a job they can name, a decision it creates, a price that feels
fair? Strict domination and the fielded/reserve numbers are the balance checks'
job; this doc asks whether a Unit is *good design*, not whether it is winning.

## Goals

* ==**every unit is someone's right answer**== — each role is the best deploy in some real board state; none is always the answer, and none never is.
* ==**pay for what it's worth**== — a Unit's Field-score cost tracks the value it brings, so no Unit is a liability to deploy or a bargain that crowds the others out.

## Axes of evaluation

1. ==**The stats tell the same story the board does.**== Read the stats table, then watch a skirmish: is Infantry the line, Cavalry the killer, Artillery the multiplier *on the board*, or only on paper? When the two disagree — a "striker" that never lands a kill, a "support" piece that wins by standing in the line — name which to move, the numbers or the role, because a Unit whose job is only in its stat line has no job.
2. ==**Fielding it asks the opponent a question.**== A good Unit landing on the board changes what the other side has to think about — a threat they must answer, a hex they can no longer ignore, a line they now have to break rather than walk round. Say what question this Unit asks that the other roles don't. If it asks none — its arrival is just more of the same, answered the same way — it is a stat block, not a piece, and the fix is to give it a thing only it can threaten.
3. ==**The price reads as a choice, not a sum.**== A player deciding whether to deploy should feel a real tension — board now against Field score later — not a calculation with one answer. Describe the moment of hesitation this Unit produces, or its absence: a piece nobody hesitates over is either free (deploy by reflex) or hopeless (stays home), and either says the cost has stopped being a decision. The fix is the cost, and the finding says which way.
